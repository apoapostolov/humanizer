# Scientific and venue register

Load when drafting papers, cover letters, grant narratives, discussion
sections, peer-review responses, or other scholarly prose. Do not run
`humanizer` blog voice over these genres.

## Scientific

- Domain terms are precision, not jargon. Keep "weighted interval score."
  Ban business buzzwords leaking in.
- Use "we" for the authors' own work. Cite named authors. Never
  "researchers have shown."
- Keep scientific formality: neither "It has long been recognized" nor
  blog "you."
- Replace "despite these challenges / growing body of literature /
  highlighting the need" with the specific implication.
- Cut trailing participle analyses that attach fake significance
  ("highlighting its importance").
- Do not coin mid-paper labels ("the supervision paradox") unless the
  term is established.
- Do not use false ranges ("from X to Y") when X and Y share no scale.
- Name the researchers or the method. Do not let an inanimate result
  "navigate" or "contribute meaningfully."
- Apply stricter preservation: keep force-bearing "never", "must", and
  "all" in safety, legal, medical, and scientific rules. Do not upgrade
  "about 50%" to "50%". Keep both bounds of ranges. Do not drop list
  items.

## Venue sampling

- Before drafting public Human prose, sample 2 to 3 recent human
  artifacts from the same venue when they are available. The venue corpus
  defines register. No corpus means domain baseline, not a generic
  "human" voice.
- Match the venue. Do not overshoot into forced casualness.
- Conventional containers (changelog categories, RFC sections, runbook
  and issue templates) are not slop. Slop is filler inside them.
- Weight checks by shape: articles -> relevance, density, stance. Short
  answers -> factuality, specificity, templatedness.
- Fluency: grammatically correct but unsayable fails. Rewrite in
  speech-shaped syntax for Human register only.

## Tech articles and openings

- Problem before topic. Include one real dead end. State one opinion plus
  the condition that would change your mind.
- If there is no real incident, the genre is notes, not a fabricated war
  story.
- Numbers carry conditions. Code is runnable or labeled a sketch.
- Opinion pieces: a claim no one could argue against is not an opinion.
  Named target plus defensible stance. Skip this on reference docs.
- For published openings only, generate a few different hooks, pick one,
  and keep the reason internal. Do not dump the tournament.

## Related
